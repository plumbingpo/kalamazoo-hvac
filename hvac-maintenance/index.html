Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • New noises when the equipment starts up
  • Musty air when the fan starts, or a filter that has been in far too long
  • A newer unit with warranty terms that call for regular professional service
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • Nobody has opened up the system in over a year
  • A failure last year that seemed to come out of nowhere
  • The house taking longer to reach the temperature you set

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and checking the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix the season before they fail.

How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of vegetation and rinse the coil gently each spring
  • Book spring and fall visits in the shoulder seasons, before the rush and before the extremes
